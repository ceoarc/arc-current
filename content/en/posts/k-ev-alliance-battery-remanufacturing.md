Three South Korean companies — EV diagnostics and logistics firm Wica Mobility, charging platform operator Softberry, and EV maintenance and battery remanufacturing company Futurex — signed a three-way strategic MOU on August 6 at Softberry's headquarters to build what they're calling the "K-EV Alliance." The goal is to connect charging, diagnosis, maintenance, battery remanufacturing, and used-car resale into a single chain for EV owners.

Each company has so far operated in a separate part of that chain. Wica Mobility runs its own diagnostic platform, WESCAN, which inspects vehicle condition, uses AI to estimate value, and handles vehicle transport and resale. Softberry operates the EV Infra charging platform, offering mobile reservations, payment, and electronic diagnostic reports and warranties. Futurex runs hub-based diagnostic centers that handle high-voltage battery replacement and powertrain maintenance, and operates Eco ChainX, a platform for remanufacturing used batteries.

## Why the pieces needed connecting

A persistent complaint in Korea's used-EV market is that buyers have no reliable way to verify battery condition. Charging history, diagnostic data, repair records, and remanufacturing history each sit with a different company, leaving buyers without a solid basis for judging a battery's remaining value. Wica Mobility CEO Jeong Tae-young said the partnership was meant to "reduce information asymmetry in the used-EV market and lay the groundwork for standardized service."

## Remanufacturing folded into the chain

What stands out in this MOU is that it pulls battery remanufacturing into the same chain as diagnosis and repair, rather than treating it as a separate business. Futurex's Eco ChainX remanufactures used batteries so they can be put back into service. If diagnostic data from Wica Mobility and charging history from Softberry feed into that process, there is room to build the kind of usage-condition data that would help determine when and whether a battery needs remanufacturing. The three companies say they plan to expand the partnership through non-exclusive joint marketing and business collaboration.

## What's still unclear

This is an MOU, not an operating service, and the companies have not disclosed how the data-sharing would actually work, how many batteries the remanufacturing effort might cover, or a timeline. It also remains to be seen what criteria Eco ChainX uses to determine whether a battery is fit for remanufacturing, and how data from the other two partners would factor into that call. ARC Current will be watching how this MOU turns into an actual service.

For context: used-EV battery diagnosis and remanufacturing has been a fragmented market in Korea, with diagnostic, charging, and remanufacturing data typically held by separate, unconnected businesses — which is the gap this alliance is aimed at closing.
