On October 1, a seminar on safety checks for used EV batteries and the safety management of a circular system for remanufactured batteries was held at the National Assembly members' office building in Seoul. It was hosted by Rep. Ahn Ho-young of the ruling Democratic Party and organized by the Korea Automobile & Mobility Safety Society and the law firm DR & AJU, with working-level officials from the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport (MOLIT) and the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment on the panel.

Some context for readers outside Korea. Two laws now frame the used-battery market here: a dedicated Act on the Management and Industrial Promotion of Used Batteries, taking effect in May 2027, and an amended Motor Vehicle Management Act that sets up a separate track for remanufactured EV batteries. Both leave most of the operational detail to enforcement decrees and ministerial rules that are still being drafted. A climate ministry official said during the panel that his ministry is working through those rules with MOLIT and the trade ministry.

Everything below is what speakers said at the seminar, not adopted policy. Five outlets reported the event consistently. That confirms the remarks were made; it says nothing about whether they are right or will end up in the rules.

## Background: treating the battery as an asset

On September 22, a National Assembly forum discussed amending the Motor Vehicle Management Act so that an EV's body and its battery could be owned and registered separately, with diagnostics and valuation floated as new business models. Treating a battery as a standalone asset first requires an agreed way to grade its condition. That grading step was the subject of the October 1 seminar.

## Can two labs get the same number?

Kim Jung-dong, a lawyer at DR & AJU who gave the keynote, described performance assessment as the fork that sends a battery toward remanufacturing, second-life reuse or recycling. His first requirement was reproducibility: the same battery under the same conditions should produce the same result, which means aligning state of charge and temperature, calibrating equipment and running inter-lab comparison tests. Prof. Lee Jong-wook of Korea University of Technology and Education added that remaining-life estimates for a single battery shift with the measurement method.

On the shop floor, this comes down to test conditions. Change the current rate, temperature or rest time and the same pack returns a different capacity figure; we covered the mechanics in [SOH estimation methods and their limits in the field](https://current.arc.ai.kr/en/posts/soh-estimation-methods-field-limits/). For a battery sitting near a grade boundary, which lab runs the test could decide which path it takes.

## Staged checks and access to BMS data

Kim laid out three stages: performance assessment before removal from the vehicle, a safety test after remanufacturing, and a pre-market safety inspection once the battery is installed in a vehicle. The session chair, Ha Sung-yong, president of the safety society, stressed that a remanufactured battery is not cleared by a bench test alone; the vehicle's electrical and thermal management has to be checked with the battery in place. Separately from what was said on the panel, the amended Motor Vehicle Management Act already writes a similar staged structure into law, with the detailed criteria for each stage delegated to MOLIT ordinances ([The certification path for remanufactured batteries](https://current.arc.ai.kr/en/posts/remanufactured-battery-certification-path/)). If in-vehicle verification gets the weight the panel gave it, remanufacturers may need procedures for checking a battery integrated with the vehicle, not just pack-level test benches.

How much battery management system (BMS) data manufacturers must share determines whether meaningful diagnosis is possible at all. Kim argued for using BMS information to the extent needed to confirm safety while protecting trade secrets. According to ZDNet Korea, a representative of the Korea Transportation Safety Authority said in the Q&A that about 35 data items manufacturers would provide are under review for the subordinate rules, but that the scope should be agreed with automakers in advance rather than imposed by law. Without charge-discharge history, cell imbalance and fault logs, assessors are left with external testing alone, which takes longer and costs more.

## When transport costs more than the battery is worth

One of the most practical remarks came from Lee Jun-kyu, a deputy director at the climate ministry. He said that if transport requirements become so strict that compliance costs exceed the battery's economic value, the system may simply not work, and that the ministry is weighing this.

The remark is worth reading against the grading structure. Assessment decides the path, but every path involves moving and storing the battery. If that cost exceeds a battery's residual value, volumes could drift toward whichever route is cheapest to process, or collection could stall, whatever the grade says. It can be read as a warning that a finely tuned grading system might end up with less influence over the eventual route. Facility and equipment standards for storage and transport are themselves among the matters delegated to subordinate rules.

## Overlapping requirements

Kim also argued that vehicle safety, resource-circulation, and fire and transport safety laws should not each demand the same or similar facilities and equipment. It sits at a different level, but it echoes a question we left open in our piece on the remanufacturing certification path: whether one company must register both under the Used Batteries Act and under the Motor Vehicle Management Act.

## What to watch

What the coverage and the remarks show is that these four issues are being raised by speakers ahead of subordinate rulemaking. When draft rules are published, ARC Current will check the primary text for how tightly test conditions are fixed, which data items manufacturers must provide, and how transport and storage standards are balanced against battery value.
