Korea's Act on the Management and Industrial Promotion of Used Batteries (Act No. 21683) takes effect on May 27, 2027, and the industry often calls it the "integration act." The nickname suggests that existing certifications and permits, from the reused-battery safety inspection to the vehicle-law grading and inspection track to waste-treatment permits, will be folded into one statute. The text does not work that way. This piece is for reuse and remanufacturing businesses asking what happens to their current certifications and permits once the law is in force, and it separates what the statute settles from what it leaves open. Article references are to the enacted text on Korea's national legislation database.

## Where the "integration act" label came from

The label started with the government. On July 10, 2024, a joint plan from several ministries, presented at a ministerial economic meeting, referred to a working-title bill on used-battery industry promotion and supply-chain stability as the "integration bill." What the government meant was gathering programs spread across ministries (history tracking, recycled-content certification, pre-removal grading, safety inspection, a policy committee) under one legal framework. The same plan said operational details would stay in each ministry's own laws and joint notices. It was never meant to replace those laws.

The National Assembly later merged several bills into a committee alternative, promulgated on May 26, 2026. The word "integration" appears nowhere in the enacted title or articles, and the official short name is simply the Used Battery Act.

## What it does not absorb

Article 4 says that where another law has a specific provision on managing used batteries, that law applies, and this act fills in only where none exists. It does not override other statutes. The supplementary provisions consist of an effective-date clause alone; there are no amendments to the Electrical Appliances and Consumer Products Safety Control Act or the Wastes Control Act. The legal basis for the [KC 10031 reused-battery safety inspection](https://current.arc.ai.kr/en/posts/kc-10031-reused-battery-inspection/) is untouched.

The link to the Motor Vehicle Management Act is a hand-off. Grading of EV drive batteries follows Article 35-13 of that act (Article 14(2)), and pre-market and periodic inspections of products carrying remanufactured batteries follow its Article 35-16 (Articles 15(2) and 16(2)). We covered that track in [The Certification Path for Remanufactured Batteries](https://current.arc.ai.kr/en/posts/remanufactured-battery-certification-path/).

## What it creates

There are new obligations, though. First, grading for "other energy storage batteries," meaning batteries in products designated by presidential decree. Owners must as a rule have the battery graded before removing it, though a presidential decree may allow grading after removal in specified cases (Article 14(1)). The Minister of Trade, Industry and Resources grades it against ministerial standards and notifies the result (Article 14(3)–(4)).

Second, inspection of "inspection-subject products": products, designated by presidential decree, that carry remanufactured or reused batteries. Sellers must obtain a pre-market safety inspection before sale (Article 15(1)), and owners must undergo a periodic safety inspection every three years from that date (Article 16(1)).

Selling without the pre-market inspection carries up to one year in prison or a fine of up to KRW 10 million (Article 30(5)). Skipping the periodic inspection, or removing an other energy storage battery without grading, carries an administrative fine of up to KRW 3 million (Article 31(1)(3)–(4)). For the two inspections, products carrying remanufactured batteries as defined in the Motor Vehicle Management Act are carved out of these penalties and stay under that act.

## Registration and definitions

Only two types of business register with the trade ministry under this act: distributors (Article 6) and reuse businesses (Article 10). Remanufacturers and recyclers do not register under it; they are defined by existing registrations and permits. For EV-type batteries, a remanufacturer is a business registered as a component manufacturer under Article 35-15 of the Motor Vehicle Management Act (Article 2(4)), and a recycler is a business holding a waste-treatment permit under the Wastes Control Act (Article 2(6)). See our [glossary](https://current.arc.ai.kr/en/posts/used-battery-glossary/) for the categories and [What to Check Before Reviewing a Reused-Battery Business in Korea](https://current.arc.ai.kr/en/posts/reused-battery-business-review-order/) for where they fall in a business plan.

There is one gap in the text. The act's definition of "battery" covers drive batteries in electric construction and agricultural machinery (Article 2(1)(a)), but the remanufacturer definition ties the vehicle-law registration only to batteries from electric, hybrid and fuel-cell vehicles. The statute does not say what registration a business remanufacturing construction or agricultural machinery batteries needs. Whether that is deliberate or will be handled elsewhere cannot be determined from the text.

## What remains undecided

None of the following is answered by the statute. All of it is open as of this writing.

The deeming clause and KC 10031. Articles 15(1) and 16(1) each provide that a product that has passed an "equivalent inspection" under another law is treated as having passed this act's inspection. The statute does not say whether the reused-battery safety inspection under the electrical safety act counts as equivalent. If it does, the existing inspection could stand in for the new one; if not, reuse businesses may need both. Which way it goes is not yet known.

Scope and registration standards. Which products are inspection-subject products, and the facility and staffing standards for distributors and reuse businesses, are all delegated to presidential decree.

Subordinate rules. A search of the government's legislative notice portal on October 6, 2026, including closed notices, turned up no draft enforcement decree or rules for this act. We also opened all eight 2026 notices for the Motor Vehicle Management Act's enforcement decree and rules; none sets detailed criteria for pre-removal grading or pre-market inspection of remanufactured batteries. In May the trade ministry said only that it would prepare them promptly.

## Effective dates differ by provision

The act takes effect on May 27, 2027 as a rule, but provisions tied to remanufacturing, including the remanufacturer definition (Article 2(4)) and the hand-off clauses (Articles 14(2), 15(2), 16(2)), take effect on July 1, 2027, the date the amended Motor Vehicle Management Act takes effect. The recycled-content target system (Article 23) starts on a date set by presidential decree within two years of the act's effective date. The remanufacturing links therefore start about a month after the distribution and reuse obligations.

## ARC's view

Read article by article, what this act integrates is less the certifications and permits than the framework for coordinating policy. A policy committee under the Ministry of Finance and Economy deliberates on amendments to related laws and notices (Article 5), and the legal basis for history tracking (Article 20) and a public trading system (Article 17) now sits here. Nothing in the text reduces existing certifications or permits, and reused products that are designated as inspection-subject products gain a periodic inspection. That much is ARC Current's reading of the statute's structure. For reuse businesses, the point that will decide the real burden is whether the deeming clause accepts KC 10031, and that is what we will check first once the subordinate rules are published.
